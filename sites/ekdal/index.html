The oak in a Långbord table grew for a century before we met it.

We only buy trees felled for forest health in Småland, then air-dry the boards for two years before a single cut. Every piece ships with the coordinates of its tree.

Knots, grain and colour decide which board becomes the backrest and which becomes the legs.

A hand plane leaves a surface no machine can: it catches the light like silk and feels warm.

Curves are worked with spokeshaves and card scrapers, by eye, until the edges feel inevitable.

Six grits, always with the grain, finished with a cabinet scraper and a cotton cloth.

Pegged mortise and tenon joints, then three coats of hardwax oil. No screws, no metal.
