The story, in plain words

AI agents can do crazy things now. They can work through your inbox, follow up with leads, research a topic and turn it into a doc, build a deck. But most people still feel behind. That's not on them.

Most AI tools are built for people who already know how they work. You open a new tab, you re-explain your whole situation, you copy things back and forth, and you still end up doing the actual work yourself.

Strawberry meets people where they already work: the browser, the most used and most intuitive interface there is. The agents live on your computer and work in the tabs and tools you already use, one click away, with all your context. If you can use a browser, you can use AI agents.

We're not replacing people. We're taking away the work that makes us feel like robots.

Strawberry is built in Stockholm and backed with $6M from EQT and General Catalyst, plus the founders of Lovable, Supabase and Hugging Face.

Saying it right

  • Call it an AI agent platform, or just "Strawberry". Lead with what the agent did for you.
  • Its edge in one line: it's right where you work, one click away, with your tabs, accounts and context. No new tab where you explain everything again.
  • Words we don't use: "AI browser", "AI employees", "early access".
  • No numbers about the company (users, sign-ups, revenue), no launch dates, no speed or benchmark claims. Show what happened on your screen instead.
  • Only claim what you saw it do. If something took two tries, that's fine to show. Honest beats perfect.

What to film

Pick a real task from your own life or job, the kind people hate doing. The best videos show one task done end to end in real tabs, and a finished thing on screen early. These are ideas, not scripts: change the task to fit your audience.

Inbox triageGmail / Outlook

Ask Strawberry to go through your inbox: sort what matters, draft replies to the ones that need you, and list what can wait.

On screen

The messy inbox, the request in plain words, the agent opening emails in your real mail tab, then the short list and the drafted replies.

Sales follow-upsCRM, LinkedIn, email

Have it read your last conversations with a few leads and write a personal follow-up for each, sent only after you say yes.

On screen

The lead list, the agent reading each thread, a draft that references something real, you saying "send it", the sent message.

Recruiting outreachLinkedIn, sheets

Give it a role. It finds candidates who fit, puts them in a sheet with why each one fits, and drafts a first message for each.

On screen

The job description, the sheet filling up row by row, one drafted message read out loud.

Research to a docweb + docs

Ask a real question (a trip, a purchase, a competitor, a topic for school or work). It reads the sources and writes a clean doc with links.

On screen

The question, tabs opening as it researches, then scroll the finished doc. Open one source to show it's real.

Meeting notes to action itemsnotes, calendar, email

Hand it the notes or transcript from a call. It pulls out who does what by when, drafts the follow-up email and puts the next meeting in the calendar.

On screen

The wall of notes, then the clean action list, the follow-up draft and the calendar invite.

A routine that runs every morningroutines

Set up something that runs on its own every weekday: a morning brief from your inbox and calendar, a news digest for your niche, a check on your leads.

On screen

You saying "do this every weekday at 8", then the next morning: the brief already waiting. "I woke up and this was done" is the moment.

Slides in your branddecks

Give it your logo, colours or website, and a topic. It builds a deck that looks like yours, then you click through it.

On screen

The brand input, a few seconds of it working (cut the wait), then three or four finished slides.

A companion that knows how you workcompanions

Create a named agent with a job (a study coach, a content assistant, a sales helper). It remembers what you told it last time.

On screen

Creating it in one prompt, giving it a task, then a day later it remembering yesterday without you explaining again.

"Comment a boring task"series

Ask your viewers for the most boring task from their job, then make each answer its own video, done live by the agent.

On screen

The comment on screen, the task in real tabs, the finished result. Every reply is the next episode.

"I gave an agent my job for a day"series

Your real job, or a job your audience has (recruiter, realtor, student, shop owner). What it did well, and honestly what it got wrong.

On screen

A quick montage of tasks across your real tools, then the scorecard.

How to show it working

Record the screen, don't film it

  • Use a real screen recording (on a Mac: Cmd + Shift + 5). Filming a monitor with your phone makes text unreadable, and viewers scroll past.
  • Zoom the browser in (Cmd and +) so text reads on a phone. Close other tabs, turn on Do Not Disturb.
  • Want your face in it? Film yourself separately and put it in the corner or cut between you and the screen.
  • Vertical 9:16, burned-in captions, clear audio. Many people watch muted.

Make it believable

  • Show the agent doing the task in real tabs: your real mail, your real sheet, a real site. That's the whole point of Strawberry.
  • Before and after: the messy starting point, then the finished result. The result is the story.
  • Cut the waiting. If you speed something up, say so on screen.
  • For "it actually sent it" moments, keep one continuous take from request to result.
  • Blur real names, emails and anything private. Use a test inbox or fictional data if you need to.
The most common miss: Strawberry only shows up in the last few seconds, or sits there like a prop. Let it do something real, early.

Questions creators ask

What is Strawberry, in one sentence?

An AI agent platform: agents that live on your computer and do real work in the browser and tools you already use, one click away with all your context.

How is it different from ChatGPT or Claude?

ChatGPT and Claude are great to talk to, but you open a new tab, explain everything, and then do the work yourself. Strawberry works right where you already are, in your tabs and accounts, and does the task: it reads the thread, writes the email, fills the sheet, builds the deck.

And from personal agents you text, like Grokbot?

Those are chats, like texting a friend, and they run on a computer somewhere else that doesn't know your context. Strawberry is built for work: you work alongside it, like a coworker, on your own computer with your own tools.

Do I need to code?

No. You type what you want in plain words, the same way you'd ask a person. If you can use a browser, you can use it.

What do I click first?

Download Strawberry at strawberrybrowser.com and sign in. Connect the tools your task needs (Settings, then Integrations: Gmail, Calendar, Slack and so on). Then open a new chat, describe the task in plain words and press Enter. Try one real task off camera first so you know how it behaves.

Which plan do I need?

Start on the free plan; it's enough to try it and film your first video. Don't buy anything for this. If you run out while filming, tell us in your email thread and we'll sort it out.

Is it a browser?

It looks like one, because the browser is where people already work, and that's what makes it easy. But what you're showing is the agents: what they do for you.

Do I have to follow one of these ideas?

No. Make the video you'd make anyway, in your own style, with Strawberry doing something real in it. The ideas are here so you're never stuck.
